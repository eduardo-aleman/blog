I recently reopened The Captive Mind by Czesław Miłosz, a scathing indictment of Stalinism and the destructive power it had on the minds of Eastern European intellectuals. The book is an engaging read, full of anecdotes, irony, and dark humor. Its power to translate a whole set of psychological experiences and subtleties that otherwise only people who have lived in a communist country could understand is truly magnificent. It also serves as a cautionary tale about totalitarian narratives and their impeccable logic.

Totalitarian narratives restrict thought and imagination while extracting, in a staggered, insidious, and cannibalistic way, deeply personal and idiosyncratically embedded moral concessions. Humans in Western democratic societies are also made to concede, but the narratives come in so many fragments that the mind can happily jump, like a rabbit, from one to another.

This much richer portfolio of fragmentary narratives propelled by the law of supply and demand is under serious threat in Donald Trump's United States as dark forces unite. MAGA, white supremacy, and an entire army of Christian zombies are coming in full force, threatening to make miserable everything and everyone. Those who are most afraid and most critical, like myself, might indeed be fixating a bit too much on them, instead of focusing on creative and practical ways to get out of the mess we, personally and collectively, are in. But then again, what if they come knocking on your door?

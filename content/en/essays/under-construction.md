I recently reopened *The Captive Mind* by Czesław Miłosz, a scathing indictment of Stalinism and the destructive power it had over the minds of Eastern European intellectuals. The book is an engaging read, full of anecdotes, irony, and dark humor. Its power to translate a whole set of psychological experiences and subtleties that otherwise only people who have lived in a communist country could understand is truly magnificent. It also serves as a cautionary tale about totalitarian narratives and their impeccable logic.

Totalitarian narratives restrict thought and imagination while extracting, in a staggered yet insidious manner, moral concessions. It is true that humans in modern Western democratic societies are also forced to concede, but the narratives at least come fragmented, not quite matching one another. The mind has more air to breathe.
